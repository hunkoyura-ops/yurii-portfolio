Why it exists

After creator outreach, dozens of replies land in the inbox. Some creators are interested, some negotiate the price, some decline, some write off-topic. Sorting them by hand is half an hour a day of reading and copying into a spreadsheet, and the hot replies get lost among the rest.

The flow, step by step

  1. Gmail picks up a new email with the creators label.
  2. Claude classifies the intent, sets a priority and extracts the creator's name, quoted prices, deadlines and questions.
  3. A switch branches by category.
  4. For substantive replies, Claude writes a draft answer. Declines and spam skip this step.
  5. The result is logged to a Google Sheet.
  6. The draft is saved to Gmail Drafts.

Decisions I made

  • A draft, not an auto-send. Automating the last step is easy, but a mistake in a conversation with a creator costs more than the minute saved.
  • Direct API calls instead of ready-made nodes. Claude is called through an HTTP request, which gives full control over the prompt and parameters.
  • Errors are handled, not hoped away. If the model returns something other than JSON, the flow logs the problem and keeps running. The switch has a fallback for an unexpected category.
  • No invented terms. The model is forbidden to make up budgets, deadlines or conditions that are not in the email. What is missing gets marked as needing clarification.
